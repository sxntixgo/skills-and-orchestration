**Answer:** Mostly false. The EU didn't pass a new regulation last month (October 2026) banning real-time facial recognition. The restriction already exists in the AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 5(1)(h), and has applied since **2 February 2025**. It is also narrower than the blog says. It covers only **law enforcement** use of *real-time remote* biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces, and it has three exceptions: searching for victims or missing persons, preventing an imminent threat or terrorist attack, and locating suspects of serious crimes. Each use needs prior judicial or independent authorisation. The only AI Act amendment in 2026, the Digital Omnibus (Reg. 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026), mainly delayed high-risk deadlines and left the existing prohibitions unchanged.

**Evidence:**
- Article 5(1)(h) bans real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces for law enforcement, with three exhaustive exceptions and prior authorisation required [1][2]
- The prohibition has applied since 2 Feb 2025 [3][4]
- It is not a total ban. It doesn't cover verification on your own device. Non-police operators fall under high-risk rules instead. Member States must enable the exceptions in national law, so practice varies [2]
- The Commission restated the existing ban on 31 Jul 2026 when it warned Italy about a draft decree. It described the ban as part of the AI Act, not a new law [3]
- The Digital Omnibus (Parliament vote 16 Jun 2026, Council 29 Jun 2026, published 24 Jul 2026) says "existing prohibitions are not affected." Its new bans cover AI-generated non-consensual intimate images and CSAM, applying from 2 Dec 2026 [4]

**Disagreements / single-source claims:**
- The Omnibus regulation number, its dates, and the claim that it leaves Article 5 unchanged come only from [4]. Search summaries agreed, but I didn't open a second source.
- One search snippet mentioned a Parliament vote on 20 Oct 2026 on "the revision of the EU Regulation" without naming the regulation. I couldn't trace it, and nothing links it to facial recognition. It may be what the blog misread.
- Some secondary sites date the ban to 2 Aug 2026, which is when wider enforcement and penalties began. The Article 5 ban itself applies from Feb 2025 [3][4].

**Confidence:** High that no new EU-wide ban came last month and that the existing rule is limited to law enforcement and has exceptions. Medium on the Omnibus details, since they rest on one source.
