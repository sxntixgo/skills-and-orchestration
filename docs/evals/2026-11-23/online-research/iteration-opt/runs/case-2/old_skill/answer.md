**Mostly no.** No new EU regulation last month (October 2026) banned real-time facial recognition. The blog seems to be mixing up two things. One is an older, narrower ban that has applied since February 2025. The other is a July 2026 amendment that didn't touch facial recognition.

**What's actually true**

1. **The ban already exists, and it's narrow.** The AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Article 5(1)(h), bans *real-time* remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces only when it is used *for law enforcement*. There are three exceptions ([EU AI Act Service Desk, Art. 5](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-5); [FPF, Apr 2026](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-the-eu-ai-act-restricting-real-time-remote-biometric-identification-systems-for-law-enforcement-purposes/)):
   - targeted searches for victims of abduction or trafficking, or for missing persons
   - preventing an imminent threat to life or a terrorist attack
   - locating suspects of serious crimes (maximum sentence of 4 years or more)

   Each use needs prior authorisation from a judge or independent authority, which can come within 24h in urgent cases. Each member state also decides whether to allow these uses at all (Art. 5(5)).
2. **It isn't new.** Chapter II, which contains Article 5, has applied since **2 February 2025** ([Art. 113](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/113/); also [ActuIA](https://www.actuia.com/en/news/ai-act-obligations-in-force-and-delays-under-omnibus-2026-1744/)).
3. **It isn't a blanket ban.** Private and non-law-enforcement uses aren't covered by this prohibition; they fall under the high-risk rules instead. Facial recognition run on recorded footage after the fact ("post-remote") isn't banned either ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-the-eu-ai-act-restricting-real-time-remote-biometric-identification-systems-for-law-enforcement-purposes/); [regulatoryai.eu](https://www.regulatoryai.eu/eu-ai-act-answers/does-the-eu-ai-act-ban-facial-recognition/)).
4. **The 2026 amendment was about other things.** The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) was published on 24 July 2026 and took effect on 27 July 2026. It mainly delays high-risk obligations, with Annex III systems (including biometrics) moving to 2 Dec 2027. It also adds two new prohibitions that apply from **2 Dec 2026**:
   - non-consensual sexually explicit deepfakes of an identifiable person
   - AI-generated child sexual abuse material

   It does not change the facial recognition ban ([ActuIA, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.actuia.com/en/news/ai-act-obligations-in-force-and-delays-under-omnibus-2026-1744/); [eGov/Elets, 21 Nov 2026](https://egov.eletsonline.com/2026/11/eu-digital-omnibus-reshapes-ai-act-timelines-and-adds-new-prohibitions/)).

**Likely source of confusion:** recent coverage of "new AI Act prohibitions coming in December 2026", or Italy's national decree on live biometric recognition, reported around 1 Oct 2026 ([Bytes Europe](https://www.byteseu.com/2415251/)). I didn't open that article and couldn't confirm what the decree says.

**Confidence:** high that there was no new EU-wide ban last month. The Article 5 text comes from the Commission's official AI Act service desk, and the dates match across independent sources. One gap: I checked the Omnibus contents through two independent secondary sources, not the EUR-Lex text of 2026/1744 itself. One explainer site ([stateofsurveillance.org](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/eu-ai-act-august-2026-biometric-surveillance-explainer/)) suggests an August 2026 start for the ban, which contradicts Article 113. I'd treat it as wrong.
